Two IKEAs
How IKEA's global model split under housing affordability pressure

By Pengcheng Lu & Samantha Wang

Act 1 · Origin
One IKEA, one model

For 60 years IKEA followed one global playbook — drive to a suburban warehouse, flat-pack, self-assemble.

In 1958, when Ingvar Kamprad opened the first IKEA store in Älmhult, Sweden, the bet was simple: families would drive to a large store, walk through a showroom, choose affordable furniture, carry flat-packed boxes home, and assemble them themselves. The three maps on the right show IKEA's footprint across North America, Europe, and East Asia — every dot the same kind of big-box suburban store.

Act 1 · Six decades
From 1958 to 2026, side by side

After 2018 a new shape appears in city centres — but only in some regions. After 2022 some of the dots dim out.

Watch the year tick forward. The Europe and North America maps fill with squares (big-box). After 2018 circles appear — city stores — and they stay open. The East Asia map tells a different story: city stores arrive a few years later (Shanghai Jing'an 2020, Tokyo Harajuku 2020, Shinjuku 2021) and several of them dim out in 2023 and 2026 as IKEA closes them. Same brand, three very different trajectories.

Act 2 · East Asia
Same pressure, no city stores

5 of the 8 highest-PTI East Asian cities never received a city-format IKEA at all.

The chart on the right ranks East Asian cities by Numbeo's Price-to-Income Ratio (left bars). Taipei, Hong Kong, Beijing, Shenzhen, Guangzhou — five of the eight highest-pressure cities — show no yellow circles, only faint big-box dots. Where IKEA did try city format (Shanghai Jing'an, Tokyo Harajuku & Shinjuku, Singapore Jurong), most of those stores have closed or are scheduled to close. Source: Numbeo Current Property Price-to-Income Ratio, fetched 2026-05-26.

Act 2 · West
Same pressure, every city store stayed open

In Western markets every city-format store opened since 2018 is still operating.

Same chart, Western cities. Every high-PTI Western city that got a city-format IKEA — London (Tottenham Court Rd 2018, Hammersmith 2022, Oxford Street 2025), Paris (La Madeleine 2019), NYC (Manhattan Studio 2019), Vienna (Westbahnhof 2021), SF (Market Street 2023) — still has it. Yellow circles, no triangles. Same housing pressure, opposite response shape.

Act 3 · West
The Western response: physical format + service

Western IKEA adapts through smaller urban stores, the TaskRabbit assembly partnership, and the IKEA Place AR app.

The Gantt-style timeline highlights only Western and global moves. The service partnership row is Western-only (TaskRabbit 2017). The urban format row collects six dated openings between 2018 and 2025. The lone Western entry in channel innovation is the IKEA Place AR app (Sep 2017, launched with Apple ARKit). The global Buyback & Resell programme (2020) covers both regimes.

Act 3 · East Asia
The East Asian response: platforms + price + retreat

East Asian IKEA leads on platform integration (5 channel launches) and is alone in price cuts and city-store closures.

Same timeline, East-Asian moves highlighted. Five platform launches — Japan online 2017, Korea e-commerce 2018, China web 2019, Tmall flagship Mar 2020, JD.com flagship Aug 2025 — make East Asia the channel-innovation leader (not the West, as the abstract originally framed). Two China price-cut events sit in 2024. Five city-store closures (Guiyang 2022, Yangpu 2022, Jing'an 2023, Harajuku & Shinjuku 2026) sit on the bottom row — no Western equivalents.

Act 4 · West
Why the West expands

Western markets glow cool: stronger DIY culture, higher car ownership, lower density and less competitive pressure — the original model still fits.

Same six dimensions, Western markets. Almost every cell sits in the blue range — the conditions that originally made the suburban big-box model work (strong DIY culture, high car ownership, low urban density, moderate service expectations, weak local manufacturing competition) are still the conditions Western IKEA operates under. That's why the Western response can be additive (new city stores layered on top of the existing network) rather than defensive. Coding anchored in Burt, Johansson & Thelander (2011); Burt, Dawson, Johansson & Hultman (2020); Ivarsson & Alvstam (2010). Cell-level evidence in data/processed/retail_ecology.csv (primary_sources column).

Coda
Two playbooks, side by side

Same 8 strategic priorities, almost opposite ranking. Urban-format stores fall from West rank 1 to East rank 7. Price defense and channel innovation flip the other way.

Three takeaways. (1) Same global brand, same housing pressure, very different responses. (2) The framing "West innovates on channel, East defends on price" is too clean — East Asia has actually led on platform integration while also cutting prices. (3) The East-Asian retreat from city format (Jing'an 2023, Harajuku & Shinjuku 2026) is the part of the story that wasn't visible from outside until the data made it concrete.
