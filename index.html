One IKEA, Two Regional Playbooks
How IKEA Adapts to Housing Affordability Pressure Across Western and East Asian Markets

By Pengcheng Lu & Samantha Wang

Act 1 · Origin
One global playbook

60 years, one playbook: suburban warehouse, flat-pack, self-assemble.

IKEA opened in Älmhult, Sweden in 1958. For decades, its model largely replicated across regions: large suburban stores, flat-pack furniture, self-service, and affordability. This map establishes the original global playbook before the article compares how the model adapts across regions.

Source: IKEA store locator and Wikipedia List of countries with IKEA stores.

Act 1 · Six decades, one map
Then the shape changed — but only in some regions

After 2018, city stores appear. After 2022, several East Asian city-store markers begin to fade out.

As the year advances, the map shows IKEA's footprint expanding through big-box stores, then adding city-format stores after 2018. In Western markets, most city-format stores remain visible in the dataset. In East Asian markets, city-format stores appear later, including Shanghai Jing'an, Tokyo Harajuku, and Shinjuku, but several later fade out as stores close or are scheduled to close. The next sections compare where housing pressure is highest and how IKEA's regional responses differ.

Source: IKEA Japan newsroom, Yicai Global, and Time Out Tokyo for the city-store opening and closure dates.

Act 2 · East Asia
High pressure, uneven city-store response

Where housing pressure is highest, IKEA's standard model appears less adaptable. Yet five of the eight top-PTI East Asian cities have no city-format store.

Taipei, Hong Kong, Beijing, Shenzhen, and Guangzhou rank among the highest-PTI East Asian cities, yet they have no city-format IKEA. IKEA did test city formats in Shanghai, Tokyo, and Singapore, but several of those stores later closed or are scheduled to close. This pattern suggests that high-density, high-pressure markets may require more than a simple city-store adaptation.

Source: Numbeo Current Property Price-to-Income Ratio, the standard cross-city affordability proxy.

Act 2 · West
Housing pressure, stronger city-store retention

Same pressure, opposite result: most Western city stores opened since 2018 are still open.

Using the same PTI scale, Western cities also show housing affordability pressure. But compared with East Asian markets, the city-format response appears more durable: most Western city-format stores opened since 2018 remain in operation. This suggests that IKEA's urban format may fit more smoothly within these market conditions.

Sources: IKEA UK, US, Austria and France newsroom press releases.

Act 3 · East Asian response
Platforms + price + retreat

East Asian response: platform launches appear alongside price cuts and city-store retreat.

East Asia complicates a simple "West innovates, East defends" story. The region shows strong platform and channel innovation, including online launches and marketplace partnerships. At the same time, these moves appear alongside price cuts, strategy repositioning, and city-store closures, suggesting a platform-heavy playbook shaped by price pressure and city-store retreat.

This timeline organizes IKEA actions by year and response type, allowing readers to compare when different strategies appeared. Sources: Inside Retail Asia, The Investor, ChinaDaily, IKEA China JD.com flagship coverage, Yicai Global, Lifeweek.

Act 3 · Western response
Physical format + service

Western response: urban-format stores, service partnerships, and add-on digital tools.

In our dataset, Western markets show more visible activity around physical-format adaptation and service partnerships. Urban stores, the TaskRabbit acquisition, and IKEA Place suggest a strategy that adds convenience to the original store model rather than replacing it. Global programs such as Buyback & Resell reinforce this pattern but are not Western-only.

This timeline organizes IKEA actions by year and response type, allowing readers to compare when different strategies appeared. Sources: CNN Business, 9to5Mac (IKEA Place), IKEA UK / US / Austria newsrooms, Ingka Group.

Act 4 · East Asia
Why East Asia Faces Defensive Pressure

Same pressure, different ecology: East Asia's denser cities and stronger local rivals make the original model harder to translate.

The retail ecology matrix suggests that IKEA faces stronger structural headwinds across several East Asian markets. Density is the most directly comparable metric, while service expectations and local competition are coded from documented market evidence. Together, these factors suggest that the original big-box, self-service model is harder to translate directly.

The business context reinforces this pressure: strong regional competitors such as Nitori and Hanssem, combined with IKEA China's reported revenue decline from its 2019 peak, suggest that East Asian markets require more localized adaptation.

Method note: Density is sourced from national statistics. Service expectations and local competition are coded from market reports and source notes; full scoring rules are documented separately.

Act 4 · West
Why Western Markets Still Fit the Original Model

Different ecology: lower density and weaker local competition help the original model remain more viable.

Compared with East Asian markets, many Western markets show conditions that better support IKEA's original model. Density is lower in many store catchments, local manufacturing competition is less dominant at IKEA's price-and-format scale, and stronger DIY-oriented retail culture supports self-service furniture shopping.

These conditions help explain why the Western response can build on the existing big-box network through city stores and service additions, rather than shifting as defensively.

Method note: Density figures come from national statistics. Competition and DIY-related evidence are coded from industry reports and academic sources; full scoring rules are documented separately.

Coda · Playbook
Two playbooks, side by side

Same brand, same housing pressure, different retail ecology: one IKEA model becomes two regional playbooks.

The slope chart compares the same eight strategic priorities across two regional playbooks. Price pressure appears in both regions, but the rest of the ranking shows different strategic emphasis.

Western markets build more on the original model through the big-box network, urban-format city stores, service partnerships, and resale/circularity. East Asian markets show a more locally adaptive playbook shaped by price pressure, strategy repositioning, platform shifts, and local competition.

The takeaway: one global IKEA model becomes two regional playbooks under different housing pressures and retail ecologies.

Method Note: Priority ranks are synthesized from documented strategic events using time decay, estimated magnitude, and within-region normalization. They summarize relative emphasis, not raw business performance.
